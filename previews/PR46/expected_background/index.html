Expected background from known activities

The forward model predicts the counts in each energy bin for one set of parameters, for example the activities of the sources. Passing many samples of the parameters through it gives the distribution of the expected spectrum and of any quantity derived from it, such as an event rate or the background index at the Q-value of the 0νββ decay. The samples can come from a fit to a measured spectrum, or be drawn from the priors before there is one.

This page draws them from priors on the activities of the components of an experiment, from radioassay measurements, upper limits or decay rates, to predict its background.

Priors of the activities

activity_prior turns a measured activity, with its uncertainty, into a Gaussian cut at 0, and an upper limit into a half-normal distribution whose quantile at the confidence level is the limit. The activities have units (from Unitful.jl), and the distribution is of the activity in µBq by default:

using Unitful
activity_prior(; val = 8.1u"mBq", unc = 1.2u"mBq")  # a measurement
activity_prior(; limit = 160u"μBq", cl = 0.9)        # an upper limit at 90% confidence

read_activities reads these priors from a YAML file, which lists the components of the experiment, their quantity (mass, area or number of pieces) and the activity per unit of quantity of each simulation (simid) that stands for them. The prior of a component is the quantity times the activity per unit. A production cycle of legend-simflow has the file in its configuration directory:

prod = SimProd("prod/l1000dsg01/v1.1.0")
activities = read_activities(prod)  # Dict(component => Dict(simid => prior))

The result is a dictionary, so priors can be added or changed by hand, for example with an activity from a half-life:

# 2νββ decay of the 7.3e27 nuclei of 76Ge in 1000 kg of germanium enriched to 92%, with a
# half-life of 2.02e21 yr, with an uncertainty of 2%
a = log(2) / 2.02e21u"yr" * 7.3e27
activities[:two_nu_bb] = Dict(:hpge_icpc_bulk_2vbb => activity_prior(; val = a, unc = 0.02 * a))

Sources of the forward model

The sources of the forward model are the spectra of the simulations, read from the production cycle with the event selection of read_pdf, and its parameters are their activities. get_activity gives the prior of the activity of a simulation from the file:

simids = [:fibers_Pb214_to_Po214, :fibers_Pb212_to_Pb208, :hpge_icpc_bulk_2vbb]
pdfs = read_pdf(prod, simids; groups = [:is_ss_aoe, :not_spms_coincident])
priors = Dict(s => get_activity(activities, s) for s in simids)

The activity of a simulation that stands for several components, for example for parts of different materials, is the sum of their activities. get_activity gives an error for it, and the caller decides how to build its prior.

norm turns an activity in µBq into the number of decays in the live time. With the live time as a quantity with units, it is the number µBq × live time:

livetime = 10u"yr"
norm = ustrip(NoUnits, u"μBq" * livetime)
model, bin_edges = build_forward_model(pdfs; norm, e_low = 500, e_high = 3000, binning = 10)

The activities are drawn from their priors. The samples have the format of those of BAT.jl, so the samples of a fit can be used in the same way below:

prior = build_prior_dist(priors)
samples = (v = [rand(prior) for _ in 1:10^5], weight = ones(10^5))

Quantities derived from the samples

Any quantity that follows from the expected counts can be computed for each sample. Its values over the samples are its distribution: the posterior with the samples of a fit, or the prediction from the priors. get_predicted_counts gives the expected counts in energy ranges for each sample, in total and for each source, and a quantity is then a function of them. The ends of the ranges must be edges of the bins of the model. For example, the event rate between 500 and 3000 keV, and the counts in the $^{208}$Tl peak at 2615 keV:

using IntervalSets, Statistics
counts = get_predicted_counts(samples, model, bin_edges, 500 .. 3000)
rate = Dict(name => uconvert.(u"s^-1", c ./ livetime) for (name, c) in counts)
peak = get_predicted_counts(samples, model, bin_edges, 2610 .. 2620)
quantile(peak[:total], [0.16, 0.5, 0.84])  # median and central 68% interval

background_index gives the background index at the Q-value of the 0νββ decay: the counts in a window around it, divided by the width of the window and by the exposure, the product of the live time and of mass, the mass of the HPGe detectors of the simulation. The result is in counts/(keV kg yr). The bins of the model must cover the window, which leaves out the γ lines at 2104 and 2119 keV. A model with one bin for each part of the window is enough:

window_bins = [[1930, 2099], [2109, 2114], [2124, 2190]]
model_qbb, bin_edges_qbb = build_forward_model(pdfs; norm, binning = window_bins)
bi = background_index(samples, model_qbb, bin_edges_qbb; livetime, mass = 1000u"kg")
quantile(bi[:total], 0.9)  # upper limit at 90% probability

group_histograms sums the values of the sources into groups, sample by sample, for example by decay chain. The total of each group, for example the whole $^{238}$U background, is then a quantity like the others:

by_chain = group_histograms(bi, ["²³⁸U" => r"Pb214_to_Po214$", "²³²Th" => r"Pb212_to_Pb208$"])
quantile(by_chain[Symbol("²³⁸U")], [0.16, 0.5, 0.84])

Plots

plot_posteriors! draws the distribution of a quantity for several sources or groups, one violin per row, labelled by the names of the sources or groups or by labels. The violins show the logarithm of the values, which often span orders of magnitude. plot_posterior! draws the distribution of one of them as a histogram, with its smallest 68% interval:

import CairoMakie
fig = CairoMakie.Figure()
ax = CairoMakie.Axis(fig[1, 1]; xlabel = "Background index [counts/(keV kg yr)]")
plot_posteriors!(ax, by_chain)  # rows: total, ²³⁸U and ²³²Th
ax_u = CairoMakie.Axis(fig[2, 1]; xlabel = "²³⁸U background index [counts/(keV kg yr)]")
plot_posterior!(ax_u, by_chain[Symbol("²³⁸U")])

plot_spectrum! draws the median of the expected counts in each bin, with the band of the central 68% interval, for the total in black and for each source or group. With residuals = false, fit_axes makes a figure without the panel of the residuals, which needs data:

fig, ax, _ = fit_axes(; residuals = false)
plot_spectrum!(ax, samples, model, bin_edges; groups = ["²³⁸U" => r"Pb214", "²³²Th" => r"Pb212"])
CairoMakie.axislegend(ax)
